# Product Hypothesis

## M1.5 Core Hypothesis

When a person privately commits to a position,
then encounters contrasting human perspectives
through a structured three-minute experience,
they can discover, clarify, reinforce, or reconsider
part of their thinking.

## Evidence Categories

To evaluate this hypothesis during M1.5 validation, evidence will be gathered across six core categories:

### 1. Understanding
Participants immediately comprehend the premise, their role, the constraints, and the social truth (asynchronous, human-sourced, non-live) without confusion.

### 2. Value
Participants find exposure to contrasting human reasoning genuinely insightful and superior to passive polling or AI-generated summaries.

### 3. Trust
Participants trust the privacy of their private commitment, the authenticity of human perspectives, and the non-judgmental stance of the AI facilitator.

### 4. Reflection
Participants actively engage in reflection—demonstrating meaningful keep, refine, or change outcomes rather than rushing through without reading.

### 5. Memorability
Participants recall the core dilemma and the contrasting perspective hours or days later, describing the experience as calm and distinct from ordinary feed scrolling.

### 6. Return Intent
Participants express a desire to engage with Today's Arena on a recurring daily basis.

## Continuing Investment Triggers

We will continue investing in Mind Arena if validation demonstrates:

- Participants consistently report discovering new aspects of a dilemma ("I hadn't considered that point").
- Participants report high trust in perspective authenticity and privacy safeguards.
- Participants complete the 3-minute flow with high qualitative satisfaction without feeling pressured to change their initial stance.

## Rethinking Triggers

We will rethink the product experience if validation shows:

- Participants treat the choice as a trivia test or game to be "won".
- Participants feel pressured by the facilitator or presentation to alter their private stance.
- Participants express skepticism regarding perspective provenance or privacy boundaries.
- Participants find the 3-minute structure tedious or indistinguishable from standard polls.
