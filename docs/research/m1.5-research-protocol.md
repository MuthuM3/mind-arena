# M1.5 Research Protocol and Testing Script

**Status:** Approved protocol; operational checklist required before recruitment

**Version:** 1.0

**Study:** Mars Rescue Today’s Arena experience prototype

**Audience:** Invited adults only

## Decision this study supports

Determine whether a short, story-driven, structured encounter with real human reasoning creates a
specific, memorable consideration that participants would not have generated alone—and whether the
experience earns voluntary return intent without debate, scoring, or fabricated social activity.

This study does not validate market size, public launch safety, live rooms, local AI, cloud AI,
subscriptions, or a catalog of arenas.

## Primary hypotheses

1. Participants understand that Today’s Arena is asynchronous and human-to-human.
2. Mars Rescue creates curiosity without suggesting a morally correct choice.
3. Independent commitment makes the later perspective feel more meaningful.
4. At least one revealed human perspective introduces a specific new consideration.
5. Build, Challenge, or Question feels safer and more useful than a freeform reply.
6. Keep, Refine, and Change all feel legitimate.
7. Mind Shift is faithful, nonjudgmental, and worth saving or discussing.
8. The journey remains understandable across compact and expanded compositions and accessibility
   preferences.

## Study design

### Round A — Storyboard comprehension

- Participants: 8–10 target adults.
- Artifact: TA-01 through TA-07 storyboard pack.
- Format: 30-minute moderated remote or in-person session.
- Purpose: identify language, trust, pacing, provenance, and composition failures before coding.

### Round B — Interactive M1.5 prototype

- Participants: 12–20 new or mixed target adults.
- Artifact: backend-free Flutter prototype with reviewed local research perspectives.
- Format: 40-minute moderated session; participant completes the arena independently before
  interview.
- Purpose: evaluate behavior, completion, useful human contrast, accessibility, and emotional arc.

Do not use Round A participants as the only Round B evidence; familiarity can hide comprehension
problems.

## Participant profile

- Age 18 or older with research eligibility confirmed.
- Comfortable reading and entering short English responses for this version.
- Curious about decisions, trade-offs, collaboration, puzzles, or thoughtful discussion.
- Mix of mobile-first and desktop/laptop users.
- Include participants who use larger text, reduced motion, keyboard navigation, or assistive
  technology when available.
- No requirement to disclose political identity, protected characteristics, trauma, profession, or
  psychological history.

Exclude anyone directly involved in authoring Mars Rescue from primary comprehension measures.

## Recruitment language

> We are testing a three-minute interactive decision experience where people consider a fictional
> situation, encounter reasoning contributed by other adults, and reflect on their own choice. This
> is a product study, not a test of intelligence or morality. You may stop at any time.

Do not recruit with claims about improving intelligence, changing minds, AI superiority, or
competition.

## Research materials

- Participant information sheet
- Research participation consent
- Separate perspective-pool consent preview
- Mars Rescue content version M1.1
- Provenance-validated research perspective package
- Editorial demonstration package for controlled fallback testing
- Storyboard pack or Flutter prototype
- Moderator script and observation sheet
- Post-session interview guide
- Privacy-safe event export
- Incident and withdrawal procedure

No real participant text, consent receipt, or moderation export is committed to the repository.

## Moderator rules

- Read scripted instructions without explaining the intended answer.
- Do not praise changing a choice or imply that keeping it is failure.
- Do not identify perspective authors.
- Do not explain a confusing label until the participant has first described what they think it
  means; record the misunderstanding.
- Do not hurry a participant to meet the three-minute target.
- Intervene immediately for distress, unsafe exposure, or provenance confusion that could cause
  harm.
- Record behavior and participant language separately from interpretation.

## Round A script

### Opening

> Thank you for helping us evaluate an early product concept. We are testing the design, not you.
> Some screens may be rough. Please describe what you believe is happening and what you expect next.
> You may pause or stop at any time.

Confirm research participation consent. Do not request perspective-pool consent in Round A unless
the participant actually contributes reusable content under the Provenance Plan.

### Scene walkthrough

For every storyboard:

1. Show the scene without explanation.
2. Ask: “What do you think is happening here?”
3. Ask: “What would you do next?”
4. Ask: “What do you believe is private, human-authored, editorial, or system-created?”
5. Ask: “How does this scene make you feel?”
6. Record hesitation, incorrect expectation, trust question, and desired missing information.

After TA-07, ask the post-session questions below.

## Round B task script

### Opening

> You will try a short fictional decision experience called Today’s Arena. Complete it as you
> naturally would. I will mostly stay quiet. This is not a test of intelligence, persuasion, or the
> correctness of your choice. Think aloud only if that feels comfortable; your typed reasons remain
> private unless you separately choose to contribute them under the displayed research terms.

Confirm research participation consent and adult eligibility. Configure the assigned viewport and
accessibility condition before launch.

### Unassisted tasks

1. Find and enter Today’s Arena.
2. Explain the Mars Rescue decision in your own words.
3. Make and commit an initial private choice.
4. Review the pool-consent decision; choose freely.
5. Examine two perspectives and select one to engage.
6. Build, Challenge, or Question it.
7. Review the consequence.
8. Keep, refine, or change the choice.
9. Review and, if desired, correct the Mind Shift.
10. Decide whether to save, share, or finish without either.

The moderator assists only after the participant is blocked long enough to abandon naturally. Record
the exact point and minimum assistance required.

### Controlled failure tasks

After the primary journey, test no more than two assigned cases per participant to limit burden:

- insufficient human perspective pool;
- editorial demonstration provenance;
- interruption and resume;
- text scaling at 200%;
- reduced motion;
- keyboard-only completion;
- compact-to-expanded resize;
- malformed local package;
- report and hide;
- contribution withdrawal explanation.

Never expose real unsafe content merely to test reporting.

## Observation sheet

Record without raw response text:

- participant study ID and assigned condition;
- viewport class and input method;
- accessibility settings used;
- scene entered/completed;
- time bucket per scene;
- hesitation or assistance code;
- consent granted/declined;
- response action type;
- keep/refine/change outcome class;
- provenance comprehension result;
- specific-new-consideration result: yes/no/unclear;
- completion, abandonment, report, hide, or error code;
- return/invite action selected;
- researcher incident flag.

Freeform notes may paraphrase interaction problems but must not reproduce private participant text.

## Post-session interview

Ask in this order:

1. “What did you think Mind Arena was after the invitation?”
2. “Which parts came from another person, and which came from the system or editors?”
3. “What thought, if any, did another person introduce that you had not considered?”
4. “What did you contribute to their reasoning?”
5. “What changed, became clearer, or stayed unresolved?”
6. “Did any part pressure you to agree, change, perform, or appear intelligent?”
7. “Who did you believe could see each thing you wrote?”
8. “Did the Mind Shift describe the session faithfully? What was wrong or missing?”
9. “When would you choose this instead of AI chat, comments, or talking directly to someone?”
10. “What would make you return for another arena?”
11. “What would make you refuse to contribute?”
12. “How would you describe this to a friend without using our product language?”

Avoid “Would you use this?” as the primary signal.

## Measures and thresholds

### Round A gate

- At least 7 of 10 accurately identify the human-to-human asynchronous value.
- At least 8 of 10 distinguish human, editorial, and facilitator content after one full walkthrough.
- At least 6 of 10 want to complete the actual exchange.
- No repeated critical misunderstanding about privacy, live presence, winner, or correct answer.
- All seven scenes have a clear expected next action for at least 8 of 10 participants.

### Round B directional gate

- At least 70% complete the full exchange without moderator action that changes the task.
- At least 60% describe the encountered human perspective as useful.
- At least 50% name a specific consideration they would not have generated alone.
- At least 50% create a substantive Build, Challenge, or Question.
- At least 80% correctly identify perspective provenance and private-versus-pooled state.
- At least one-third voluntarily select a genuine return or next-arena action.
- Serious trust or safety failures: zero.
- Raw-content telemetry or logging incidents: zero.
- Critical compact/expanded, keyboard, text-scale, or reduced-motion blockers: zero before completion
  of the round.

These are small-study decision thresholds, not public product claims.

## Meaningful Perspective Exchange

A completed session counts as an MPE only when:

- the participant committed independently;
- two eligible real human perspectives were available;
- the participant engaged one through an approved structured action;
- the final reflection accurately includes that source relationship;
- no unresolved safety, provenance, or consent failure invalidates the session.

Editorial demonstration and AI-generated examples never count as MPEs.

## Stop rules

Stop the individual session immediately for:

- participant request or visible significant distress;
- accidental unsafe or unmoderated exposure;
- provenance or consent state that cannot be verified;
- adult eligibility uncertainty;
- repeated dehumanizing behavior after one neutral boundary reminder;
- a privacy or data-handling failure.

Pause the study for review when:

- any serious trust or safety failure occurs;
- editorial/AI content is mistaken for a participant by three participants after the complete flow;
- withdrawal fails or a withdrawn contribution remains selectable;
- raw content appears in analytics, logs, screenshots, or source control;
- more than 30% cannot complete because of the same interaction problem;
- accessibility blockers prevent completion in an assigned condition;
- the moderator must routinely explain the core human-to-human value.

## Data handling

- Assign opaque study IDs.
- Store research participation consent separately from perspective-pool consent.
- Do not record screen, audio, or video unless separately disclosed and necessary.
- Prefer structured observations and paraphrased notes.
- Store any raw contribution only in the protected research system governed by the Provenance Plan.
- Exclude raw content from product analytics and crash reports.
- Restrict access to the named research team.
- Honor withdrawal and deletion according to the participant information sheet.
- Report only aggregated or de-identified findings; suppress small sensitive groups.

## Analysis method

1. Review incidents and invalidate affected sessions before calculating completion.
2. Summarize behavior by scene, viewport, input, and accessibility condition.
3. Code misunderstandings using a fixed taxonomy: value, provenance, privacy, action, consequence,
   reflection, or navigation.
4. Record the participant’s specific new consideration as yes/no/unclear without storing its raw
   private wording in analytics.
5. Compare keep/refine/change outcomes without treating change as superior.
6. Separate interaction failure from content failure and perspective-selection failure.
7. Write one decision per material finding: keep, revise, retest, remove, or stop.

## Decision outcomes

- **Proceed:** gates pass and participants identify memorable human value.
- **Revise interaction:** human exchange is useful but scenes, language, pacing, or trust controls
  block completion.
- **Revise content:** flow works but Mars Rescue fails to generate safe, varied reasoning.
- **Revise distribution:** value appears only inside known communities; test a community-led wedge.
- **Stop expansion:** multiple well-run rounds cannot produce distinct human value over solo AI or
  ordinary conversation.

Do not compensate for failed human value with more animation, gamification, model capability, or
infrastructure.
