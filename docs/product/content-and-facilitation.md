# Content and Facilitation System

## Why scenarios are a core product system

The scenario determines curiosity, emotional stakes, available choices, which perspectives emerge,
what abuse is likely, and whether the exchange produces insight. A poor scenario cannot be rescued
by a better model or more animation.

V1 content is editorially curated and versioned.

## Good-arena contract

A V1 arena must:

- establish its world and decision in under 30 seconds;
- be understandable without specialist knowledge;
- create curiosity without deception;
- have at least three defensible choice/reasoning paths;
- affect something people can imagine;
- avoid requiring disclosure of private trauma or identity;
- support a useful exchange in three minutes;
- allow uncertainty;
- avoid a single obvious factual answer;
- have a bounded moderation and misinformation risk;
- work across more than one cultural or socioeconomic perspective;
- produce at least one transferable thinking habit;
- support a bounded deterministic consequence;
- produce a comparable spoiler-safe result.

## Excluded V1 topics

- elections, parties, candidates, and active political campaigns;
- medical, legal, investment, or crisis decisions;
- self-harm, eating disorders, abuse, or traumatic disclosure;
- religion or immutable identity as a proposition to judge;
- criminal accusations about real people;
- breaking news and unverified events;
- sexual content;
- personalized workplace or relationship conflicts;
- prompts designed to provoke outrage.

Exclusion is a staging decision, not a claim that thoughtful discussion of these subjects is
unimportant.

## Arena package

Each arena version contains:

- title, premise, atmosphere, and display prompt;
- roles, constraints, world state, and neutral context;
- why it matters;
- optional definitions;
- stance configuration;
- expected reasoning families;
- allowed actions and deterministic consequences;
- likely assumptions and trade-offs;
- facilitation moves;
- unsafe-content patterns;
- misinformation notes;
- perspective-diversity rubric;
- editorial fallback perspectives with explicit labels;
- Mind Shift synthesis rules;
- share-card fields;
- launch and expiry time;
- language and locale;
- review owner and status.

## Facilitation moves

The system chooses a move based on the state, not an open-ended conversation.

### Before reveal

- request a reason;
- ask for confidence;
- clarify ambiguity without influencing stance.

### During selection

- find distinct reasoning;
- avoid repetitive exposure;
- balance contributor exposure;
- filter unsafe or low-substance content.

### During contribution

- ask the participant to Build, Challenge, Question, or Connect;
- suggest a respectful rewrite;
- request specificity;
- redirect a personal attack to the underlying claim.

### During reflection

- identify a supported change;
- distinguish convergence from agreement;
- preserve unresolved questions;
- avoid inventing motives or traits.

## Perspective quality

Eligibility is not a popularity contest.

A perspective is more useful when it is:

- relevant to the question;
- understandable;
- supported by a reason;
- distinct from available perspectives;
- safe and respectful;
- specific enough to engage.

Fluent writing is not required. The product must not disadvantage non-native speakers or concise
contributors. AI may offer an optional meaning-preserving rewrite that the author approves.

## Human and AI boundaries

- Human words remain human-authored unless an approved rewrite is clearly recorded.
- Editorial cards are labeled “From the Mind Arena editors.”
- AI cards are labeled “A possible missing perspective,” never with a human alias.
- AI may summarize multiple people only when the source relationship is preserved.
- The facilitator does not invent statistics, citations, or participant consensus.

## Editorial workflow

1. Draft arena package.
2. Internal adversarial review.
3. Run 5–8 concierge sessions.
4. Revise language and facilitation moves.
5. Moderation review.
6. Limited shared-arena cohort release.
7. Inspect quality, safety, and perspective-distribution data.
8. Expand, revise, pause, or retire.

Every edit creates a version. Sessions and contributions retain their originating version.

## Content portfolio

After Mars Rescue proves the loop, expand cautiously across:

- resource dilemmas;
- mysteries and evidence;
- negotiation;
- survival and crisis simulation;
- creativity and strategy;
- fictional leadership decisions.

Every scenario must reuse the Arena Engine grammar. Do not publish a catalog before the editorial
process can sustain emotional, reasoning, moderation, and replay quality.

## Facilitator evaluation

Evaluation cases include:

- similar stances with different reasons;
- opposing stances with the same underlying value;
- low-confidence participants;
- dominant but repetitive perspectives;
- concise or imperfect language;
- sarcasm and subtle personal attacks;
- unsafe content;
- no real convergence;
- a legitimate decision not to change;
- insufficient perspective pool.

Human review scores:

- usefulness of contrast;
- fidelity;
- respect;
- neutrality;
- clarity of requested action;
- absence of invented claims;
- whether the move led to new human thinking.
