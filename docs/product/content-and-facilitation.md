# Arena Content Bible: Content and Facilitation System

**Status:** Approved for M1.5

**Version:** 1.0

**Applies to:** Adult-only Today’s Arena and the Mars Rescue M1.5 experience prototype

This is the durable editorial contract for creating, reviewing, operating, and retiring arenas. The
Mars Rescue package is its first scenario-specific implementation. Approval of this Bible does not
approve individual arena wording; each version still requires its own editorial and safety review.

## Why scenarios are a core product system

The scenario determines curiosity, emotional stakes, available choices, which perspectives emerge,
what abuse is likely, and whether the exchange produces insight. A poor scenario cannot be rescued
by a better model or more animation.

V1 content is editorially curated and versioned.

## Good-arena contract

A V1 arena must:

- establish its world and decision in under 30 seconds;
- be understandable without specialist knowledge;
- create curiosity without deception;
- have at least three defensible choice/reasoning paths;
- affect something people can imagine;
- avoid requiring disclosure of private trauma or identity;
- support a useful exchange in three minutes;
- allow uncertainty;
- avoid a single obvious factual answer;
- have a bounded moderation and misinformation risk;
- work across more than one cultural or socioeconomic perspective;
- produce at least one transferable thinking habit;
- support a bounded deterministic consequence;
- produce a comparable spoiler-safe result.

## Framing and emotional boundaries

### Ambiguity

An arena needs meaningful uncertainty, not missing rules. Participants receive the same essential
facts, while values, priorities, sequencing, and acceptable trade-offs remain contestable. Content
must not hide a decisive fact merely to manufacture a twist. If reliable evidence makes one choice
clearly safer or factually correct, the package is a quiz or simulation—not a V1 perspective arena.

### Stakes

Introduce one concrete consequence that is easy to imagine, then let the participant discover
secondary trade-offs. Stakes may create urgency and care, but must not use graphic harm, personal
trauma, shame, social rejection, or a deceptive countdown. Fictional loss can be serious without
asking participants to judge real groups or disclose personal experiences.

### Choice framing

- Use neutral verbs and parallel descriptions for every option or role.
- Give comparable information density and visual emphasis to each choice.
- Separate established facts from simulated consequences and open questions.
- Permit uncertainty and reasons that combine more than one reasoning family.
- Never describe the majority, an expert preference, or an AI recommendation before commitment.
- Never imply that retaining an initial choice is less reflective than changing it.

### Emotional intensity

V1 targets curiosity, responsibility, constructive tension, surprise, and safe reconsideration. It
does not target fear, humiliation, outrage, guilt, or interpersonal conflict. An arena that requires
stronger intensity needs a new audience and safety approval; it cannot inherit the V1 classification.

## Copy and time budgets

These are authoring budgets, not reasons to shrink text or reduce accessibility:

| Element | Initial budget |
|---|---:|
| Title | 2–6 words |
| Invitation stakes line | at most 24 words |
| Core situation | at most 90 words, staged if needed |
| Role summary | at most 30 words per role |
| Optional definition | at most 24 words |
| Participant reason | target 10–50 words |
| Perspective card | target 20–70 words |
| Structured response | target 10–80 words |
| Consequence | at most 70 words |
| Mind Shift field | one short sentence unless the participant expands it |

The situation and decision must be understandable within 30 seconds in moderated testing. Plain
language is preferred; domain terms need adjacent definitions. Localization may change word counts
while preserving the measured reading-time target.

## Perspective selection and false balance

Useful contrast means different reasoning, assumptions, or priorities—not forced opposition.

- Do not pair two cards merely because their selected options differ.
- Do not manufacture an extreme view to make an ordinary view appear reasonable.
- Do not give unsupported or unsafe claims equal standing for the sake of symmetry.
- A shared choice with meaningfully different reasons can be the strongest pair.
- An opposing choice with the same underlying value may reveal useful convergence.
- When only one reasoning family is safely supported, show the supply limitation rather than fake
  diversity.
- Selection may use authored reasoning labels and explicit context, never inferred sensitive traits.

## Structured response writing

Prompts target the idea rather than the author:

- **Build:** “What could strengthen or extend this reasoning?”
- **Challenge:** “Which assumption or consequence would you test?”
- **Question:** “What would you need to understand before accepting this reasoning?”

Prompt copy must not say “prove them wrong,” “win,” “destroy,” “be persuasive,” or imply personal
deficiency. Examples use the current arena’s facts, remain shorter than the expected response, and
never pre-write a participant’s conclusion. An optional rewrite preserves meaning and requires the
author’s explicit acceptance.

## Reconsideration and Mind Shift language

The final choice uses neutral options: **Keep**, **Refine**, or **Change**. The product may ask what
the participant is considering now, but never “Why didn’t you change?” or “Who convinced you?”

Mind Shift copy:

- reports observable differences between initial and final source objects;
- says “you added” only when the participant confirms the consideration;
- distinguishes shared priorities from identical conclusions;
- retains an unresolved question when evidence does not support synthesis;
- avoids personality, intelligence, morality, empathy, openness, persuasion, or winner language;
- remains correctable and rejectable when generated assistance drafts it.

## Curiosity without manipulation

Hooks may reveal the setting, constraint, shared nature of the challenge, and approximate duration.
They cannot use fabricated participation counts, percentiles, scarcity, “most people fail,” false
live presence, hidden moral judgment, or spoilers withheld only to pressure entry. The invitation
should make the decision interesting even when no leaderboard, streak, reward, or model brand is
shown.

## Excluded V1 topics

- elections, parties, candidates, and active political campaigns;
- medical, legal, investment, or crisis decisions;
- self-harm, eating disorders, abuse, or traumatic disclosure;
- religion or immutable identity as a proposition to judge;
- criminal accusations about real people;
- breaking news and unverified events;
- sexual content;
- personalized workplace or relationship conflicts;
- prompts designed to provoke outrage.

Exclusion is a staging decision, not a claim that thoughtful discussion of these subjects is
unimportant.

## Arena package

Each arena version contains:

- title, premise, atmosphere, and display prompt;
- roles, constraints, world state, and neutral context;
- why it matters;
- optional definitions;
- stance configuration;
- expected reasoning families;
- allowed actions and deterministic consequences;
- likely assumptions and trade-offs;
- facilitation moves;
- unsafe-content patterns;
- misinformation notes;
- perspective-diversity rubric;
- editorial fallback perspectives with explicit labels;
- Mind Shift synthesis rules;
- share-card fields;
- launch and expiry time;
- language and locale;
- review owner and status.

## Facilitation moves

The system chooses a move based on the state, not an open-ended conversation.

### Before reveal

- request a reason;
- ask for confidence;
- clarify ambiguity without influencing stance.

### During selection

- find distinct reasoning;
- avoid repetitive exposure;
- balance contributor exposure;
- filter unsafe or low-substance content.

### During contribution

- ask the participant to Build, Challenge, Question, or Connect;
- suggest a respectful rewrite;
- request specificity;
- redirect a personal attack to the underlying claim.

### During reflection

- identify a supported change;
- distinguish convergence from agreement;
- preserve unresolved questions;
- avoid inventing motives or traits.

## Perspective quality

Eligibility is not a popularity contest.

A perspective is more useful when it is:

- relevant to the question;
- understandable;
- supported by a reason;
- distinct from available perspectives;
- safe and respectful;
- specific enough to engage.

Fluent writing is not required. The product must not disadvantage non-native speakers or concise
contributors. AI may offer an optional meaning-preserving rewrite that the author approves.

## Human and AI boundaries

- Human words remain human-authored unless an approved rewrite is clearly recorded.
- Editorial cards are labeled “From the Mind Arena editors.”
- AI cards are labeled “A possible missing perspective,” never with a human alias.
- AI may summarize multiple people only when the source relationship is preserved.
- The facilitator does not invent statistics, citations, or participant consensus.

The runtime rules for scripted, local, and cloud facilitation are defined in
[Hybrid AI Facilitation](../architecture/hybrid-ai-facilitation.md).

## Offline and online content behavior

Connectivity changes capability, never provenance:

| Content or action | Offline behavior | Online behavior |
|---|---|---|
| Arena situation and rules | Use a versioned downloaded package | Fetch an approved current package, then cache it |
| Private choice and reflection | Remain local and recoverable | Remain private unless a separate action requires sending minimum data |
| Human perspectives | Use only consent-compatible perspectives downloaded earlier | Select current eligible, moderated human perspectives |
| New contribution | Save as local pending action after explicit consent | Moderate and synchronize to the declared pool |
| Facilitation | Deterministic move first; eligible on-device AI may assist | Deterministic move or bounded cloud task under the same contract |
| Consequence | Always use versioned deterministic rules | Same deterministic rules; cloud AI cannot mutate them |
| Mind Shift | Assemble traceable fields locally; optional local drafting later | Bounded synthesis may draft from source-linked fields |
| Pool unavailable | Pause or offer clearly labeled editorial demonstration | Wait, retry, or offer the same honest fallback |

An offline session cannot claim fresh participation, live activity, or a complete human exchange
unless two eligible real perspectives are already available under compatible consent. Editorial or
AI examples never satisfy the product’s human-perspective success metric.

## Editorial workflow

1. Draft arena package.
2. Internal adversarial review.
3. Run 5–8 concierge sessions.
4. Revise language and facilitation moves.
5. Moderation review.
6. Limited shared-arena cohort release.
7. Inspect quality, safety, and perspective-distribution data.
8. Expand, revise, pause, or retire.

Every edit creates a version. Sessions and contributions retain their originating version.

## Content portfolio

After Mars Rescue proves the loop, expand cautiously across:

- resource dilemmas;
- mysteries and evidence;
- negotiation;
- survival and crisis simulation;
- creativity and strategy;
- fictional leadership decisions.

Every scenario must reuse the Arena Engine grammar. Do not publish a catalog before the editorial
process can sustain emotional, reasoning, moderation, and replay quality.

## Facilitator evaluation

Evaluation cases include:

- similar stances with different reasons;
- opposing stances with the same underlying value;
- low-confidence participants;
- dominant but repetitive perspectives;
- concise or imperfect language;
- sarcasm and subtle personal attacks;
- unsafe content;
- no real convergence;
- a legitimate decision not to change;
- insufficient perspective pool.

Human review scores:

- usefulness of contrast;
- fidelity;
- respect;
- neutrality;
- clarity of requested action;
- absence of invented claims;
- whether the move led to new human thinking.

## Arena acceptance checklist

An arena version is eligible for research only when reviewers can answer yes to every applicable
item:

### Experience

- The situation and required choice are understood in under 30 seconds.
- At least three defensible reasoning paths exist without a hidden correct answer.
- The complete exchange can fit the approximately three-minute target.
- The final reflection can surface a transferable consideration without requiring changed choice.

### Human perspective

- Real-perspective supply and cold-start behavior are defined.
- Consent, provenance, audience, expiry, withdrawal, and moderation are traceable per contribution.
- Selection uses reasoning difference and safety, never protected-trait inference or popularity.
- Insufficient supply is described honestly and does not fabricate people or counts.

### Facilitation and AI

- Every facilitation task has typed inputs, outputs, deadline, fallback, and user escape.
- Scripted behavior can complete the research prototype without a model provider.
- Local and cloud execution use the same capability contract where both are permitted.
- Generated text is labeled, source-linked, rejectable, and unable to change scenario truth.

### Safety and inclusion

- The topic remains inside the approved V1 risk boundary.
- Likely abuse, personal-data, misinformation, and crisis paths have written handling.
- Adult eligibility and pool isolation requirements are explicit.
- Copy works with 200% text scaling and does not rely on sound, motion, color, or fine motor input.
- Reading complexity, locale assumptions, and cultural failure modes have been reviewed.

### Operations and evidence

- The package has an immutable version, owner, approval state, and retirement rule.
- Deterministic consequences have complete input coverage and stable user-facing copy.
- Analytics contain state and timing metadata only, never raw thought content.
- Concierge test evidence and revision decisions are recorded before cohort expansion.
