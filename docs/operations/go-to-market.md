# Go-to-Market Strategy

## Launch thesis

Mind Arena should launch through communities, not broad app-store discovery.

The early product needs:

- enough perspectives per arena;
- a norm of thoughtful contribution;
- fast founder feedback;
- bounded moderation;
- repeated participation.

A small dense cohort is more valuable than a large sparse audience.

## Beachhead communities

Prioritize two types:

1. builder/founder/design communities that enjoy ambiguous problems;
2. university, book, or learning cohorts with an existing discussion habit.

Recruit adults through trusted community hosts. Avoid mixing many communities until the product can
understand how norms affect contribution.

## Cohort launch

### Founding roundtable

Invite 25–50 people to help shape the interaction. Expectations are explicit:

- private beta;
- real contributions may be shown pseudonymously to cohort members;
- active founder moderation;
- limited arena schedule;
- direct research follow-up.

### Question cadence

Start with one Today’s Arena per week, not daily. Increase cadence only when:

- perspective supply is ready;
- moderation is timely;
- editorial quality remains high;
- repeat behavior supports it.

“Daily Arena” is the durable product concept, not a promise on day one.

### Invitation loop

An existing participant may invite one thoughtful person to the same or a future arena. The message
sells the situation—“What would you do?”—not a referral reward.

Do not create unlimited invites or growth incentives before moderation capacity.

## Activation

A user is activated after completing one Meaningful Perspective Exchange—not after account
creation.

Reduce friction by:

- showing the arena premise before sign-up;
- explaining the human perspective contract;
- using simple invite-linked authentication;
- keeping the first response concise;
- delaying profile/settings choices.

## Retention

Return drivers:

- a genuinely strong next arena;
- notification that someone engaged the participant’s contribution;
- a private history of Mind Shifts;
- recurring cohort identity;
- occasional live roundtable invitation.

Do not use guilt, fake urgency, or streak loss.

## Organic sharing

Safe shareable artifacts:

- arena card;
- participant’s own redacted Mind Shift;
- aggregate insight after a privacy threshold;
- invite to contribute to the next cohort.

Never expose another participant’s words or alias in a public share without explicit compatible
consent.

## Community-hosted path

If community cohorts show the strongest retention, provide hosts with:

- scheduled curated arena;
- invite management;
- safety/escalation contact;
- aggregate, privacy-safe completion view;
- shared synthesis with minimum group threshold.

Hosts do not receive private initial thoughts or individual “quality” scores.

## Launch measures

- invite → first MPE;
- eligible perspectives per arena;
- time until perspective reveal;
- second-question participation;
- response notification return;
- cohort-level usefulness/respect;
- report and moderation load;
- organic invite rate;
- host willingness to run another round.

## Expansion gate

Open beyond hand-selected cohorts only when:

- arena supply and moderation capacity are predictable;
- perspective selection remains useful at larger scale;
- safety controls and incident response are tested;
- retention is not entirely founder-driven;
- product copy accurately sets asynchronous expectations;
- no community is being exploited as free content supply.
